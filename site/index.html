Bespoke data solutions for nature recovery

One living record of your land

Camera traps, acoustic recorders, GPS tags, surveys in the field: a rewilding project generates data every day. Canopy builds the software that brings it together as it arrives, so you can see what's changing on your land and act on it.

What we do

From a shoebox of SD cards to a live platform your whole team logs into. We take on the whole journey, or any one part of it.

All your data in one place

We consolidate survey spreadsheets, consultant reports and historic records into one shared platform, so every sighting, survey and device is at your whole team's fingertips.

Bespoke applications

Software shaped around how you actually work: volunteer survey entry, camera-trap review, tracking dashboards. Built quickly, then refined in the field alongside you.

Device pipelines & AI

Camera traps, acoustic recorders and GPS tags feeding your record automatically. AI species identification you review, rather than data entry you dread.

Analysis & reporting

Seasonal trends, species discovery curves and the evidence funders, partners and regulators ask for. Generated from live data, not compiled by hand each year.

Case study

Heal Rewilding

Heal Somerset, a rewilding charity

Heal runs on volunteers, so their Canopy is shaped around them. Survey groups for butterflies and dragonflies plan their season, recorders enter counts from a phone in the field, and every walk feeds the same living record the staff team works from. It's the same platform as Cannwood underneath, with a different application on top. That's what bespoke means.

Recording in the field
Birds added to the map as you walk, by BTO code, from a phone.
The season at a glance
Species totals and trends, live from every walk; no end-of-year spreadsheet.
Surveyor management
Scheduled weeks with sign-up from a phone; everyone can see who's covering what.

Case study

Cannwood

A 400-acre rewilding estate in Somerset

Cannwood is where Canopy began: one estate's camera traps, bird surveys and historic records pulled into a single living map. Today the platform runs the estate's day-to-day ecological record. Satellite-tagged turtle doves are followed through the season, camera-trap cards are identified by AI and reviewed by eye, and decades of aerial imagery show the land changing on one timeline.

AI species ID
A card of camera-trap images identified in minutes; you review the calls, not the folders.
The land, changing
Aerial imagery on one timeline, 2000 to 2024: fields returning to woodland.
GPS tracking
Satellite-tagged turtle doves followed through the season, on the same map as the surveys.
